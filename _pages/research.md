My research focuses on compact objects and their binary evolution, with a particular interest in neutron stars, black holes, X-ray binaries, radio pulsars, and radio transients. I combine theoretical modeling with observational data analysis to understand how compact binaries form, evolve, and reveal themselves across electromagnetic bands.

Compact Binary Evolution
======

I study the formation and evolution of compact binaries containing neutron stars, black holes, and white dwarfs. A central theme of my work is how mass transfer, super-Eddington accretion, common-envelope evolution, stellar winds, and compact-object mass growth shape the observed populations of X-ray binaries, binary pulsars, and mass-gap black-hole candidates.

This work connects stellar evolution calculations with observed systems such as low-mass X-ray binaries, ultraluminous X-ray pulsars, binary millisecond pulsars, and compact-object candidates discovered in optical and spectroscopic surveys.

Radio Pulsars and Transients
======

I search for and characterize radio pulsars and transient radio sources using observations from facilities such as FAST and the Green Bank Telescope. My observational work includes periodicity searches, fast folding algorithm searches, acceleration searches, and single-pulse analyses.

I am especially interested in faint, long-period, sporadic, and high-dispersion-measure pulsars that can be missed by standard search methods. These systems provide useful probes of neutron-star emission physics, Galactic electron-density structure, and compact-object populations.

FAST and GBT Observations
======

FAST provides exceptional sensitivity for pulsar and transient searches, especially in archival observations with long integration times. I use FAST data to search for new pulsars, rotating radio transients, and radio emission from compact-object candidates.

The Green Bank Telescope is also important for targeted high-frequency pulsar searches. One example is the discovery of PSR J1750-3116A, the first radio pulsar likely associated with the globular cluster Terzan 6.

Small Radio Telescopes and Outreach
======

I am involved in observing practice and outreach with the 4.5-m radio telescope at Nanjing University's [Zuo Dijiang Observatory](/zuo-dijiang-observatory/). This work uses a small radio telescope as a hands-on platform for antenna pointing, data acquisition, signal processing, and radio-astronomy education.

One focus is showing how pulsar signals can be received and interpreted, connecting the physics of neutron stars with real radio data and practical observing experience.
